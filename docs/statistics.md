## The Minnesota figure needs a decision

The 37% figure is real, but it comes from the first phase of the study only. The same author's final evaluation, which followed both phases for longer, found a smaller effect.

- **First phase (Duwe, 2012):** the program reduced the risk of rearrest by 37%, reconviction by 43%, and reincarceration for a new offense by 57%.
- **Final evaluation (Duwe, 2013):** the program lowered the risk of rearrest by 20%, reconviction by 21%, and any return to prison by 24%. The report says the large early effects "tapered off over time" and calls the reduction "relatively modest".

A judge who checks the source will find the 20% figure. Options:

- **A (recommended):** use the final figure. "In a randomized Minnesota study, coordinated reentry support lowered the risk of rearrest by 20%."
- **B:** keep 37% and say exactly what it is. "In the first phase of a randomized Minnesota study, coordinated reentry support lowered the risk of rearrest by 37%."
- **C:** drop the number. "A randomized Minnesota study found that coordinated reentry support lowers rearrest."

The video already says "over 37%", so options A and C would make the site differ from the video.
